## Synthesis

Four independent research teams demonstrated in 2021 that individuals in verified REM sleep can receive questions from experimenters and provide correct answers in real time, while remaining asleep and in a lucid dream. Across 36 participants and 158 communication attempts during signal-verified lucid dreams, 18.4% yielded correct responses versus only 3.2% incorrect, a ratio statistically incompatible with random responding. Dreamers received math problems or yes/no questions via audio, visual (Morse-coded LED flashes), or tactile stimuli, and responded through voluntary eye movements or facial muscle contractions.

The study builds on LaBerge's 1981 foundational work but goes further by presenting novel questions unknown to participants in advance, proving genuine real-time cognition rather than conditioned responses. Dreamers preserved advanced cognitive abilities during REM sleep, including working memory, instruction recall, and arithmetic computation. External stimuli were often integrated into dream content, sometimes perceived as a radio, flickering lights, or a narrator's voice. The main limitation was a 60.8% no-response rate, attributed to sensory gating, arousal, or loss of lucidity.
