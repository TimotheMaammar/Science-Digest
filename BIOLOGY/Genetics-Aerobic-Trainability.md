# Genetics of aerobic trainability

## About

Title : Genomic predictors of the maximal O2 uptake response to standardized exercise training programs

Links :

- <https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/japplphysiol.00973.2010>
- <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21183627/>

Researchers : Claude Bouchard, Mark A. Sarzynski, Treva K. Rice, William E. Kraus, Timothy S. Church, Yun Ju Sung, D.C. Rao, Tuomo Rankinen (Pennington Biomedical Research Center)

Date : 05/2011

## Synthesis

A foundational study in exercise genetics, drawn from the HERITAGE Family Study, which subjected 473 sedentary white adults from 99 families to an identical, fully standardized 20-week aerobic training program. The central observation is that the response to the exact same training is enormously variable between individuals, and that this variability is largely genetic. The heritability of the gain in VO2max after training was estimated at 47%, meaning roughly half of how much a person improves from a given aerobic program is determined by their genes rather than by effort or program design. This reframes a common assumption: trainability itself, not just baseline fitness, is inherited.

The study then performed a genome-wide association scan across 324,611 SNPs to find the specific genetic variants behind this. A panel of 21 SNPs was identified that together explained 49% of the variance in VO2max trainability, closely matching the heritability estimate. The effect is large in practical terms: subjects carrying 9 or fewer favorable alleles improved their VO2max by an average of 221 mL/min, while those carrying 19 or more favorable alleles gained 604 mL/min, nearly a threefold difference from the same training. The single strongest variant, rs6552828 in the ACSL1 gene, accounted on its own for about 6% of the training response.

The practical and conceptual implications are significant. It explains the well-documented existence of high responders and low responders to cardio training, and even non-responders who improve very little despite full compliance. It also means population-level advice ("do X and your VO2max will rise by Y") masks wide individual variation. The genes nearest the strongest predictors (PRDM1, GRIN3A, KCNH8, ZIC4, ACSL1) point toward diverse biological pathways rather than a single mechanism, which is consistent with VO2max trainability being a complex polygenic trait. An important caveat is that the discovery sample was entirely white and relatively small for a GWAS, so the specific SNP panel has not replicated cleanly in other populations, even though the broad finding of high heritability is robust.
