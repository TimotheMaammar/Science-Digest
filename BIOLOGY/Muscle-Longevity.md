# Muscle strength, exercise capacity and cardiovascular disease

## About

Title : Exercise capacity and muscle strength and risk of vascular disease and arrhythmia in 1.1 million young Swedish men: cohort study

Links : 
- https://www.bmj.com/content/351/bmj.h4543
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26378015/
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4768156/

Researchers : Kasper Andersen, Finn Rasmussen, Claes Held, Martin Neovius, Per Tynelius, Johan Sundström

Date : 09/2015

## Synthesis

One of the largest prospective cohort studies ever conducted on physical fitness and cardiovascular outcomes, following 1.1 million Swedish men who underwent mandatory military conscription between 1972 and 1995 at a median age of 18.2 years, with follow-up until 2010 (median 26.3 years). Exercise capacity was assessed by ergometer bicycle test and muscle strength by handgrip dynamometer. During follow-up, 26,088 vascular disease events and 17,312 arrhythmia events were recorded. The key finding: high exercise capacity combined with high muscle strength was associated with a hazard ratio of 0.67 (95% CI: 0.65-0.70) for vascular events compared to low exercise capacity and low muscle strength, representing a 33% reduction in risk. Both predictors contributed independently after mutual adjustment.

The muscle strength findings are particularly notable because they hold after controlling for cardiorespiratory fitness. This means strength carries its own protective signal, not just as a proxy for aerobic fitness. Higher muscle strength was specifically associated with lower risk of heart failure and cardiovascular death, and lower risk of ventricular arrhythmia and bradyarrhythmia. The relationship between muscle strength and vascular disease appears linear, with no plateau observed across the measured range, suggesting that more is consistently better.

One important nuance concerns arrhythmia and exercise capacity specifically. Exercise capacity showed a U-shaped association with arrhythmia risk, driven by a direct association with atrial fibrillation, a well-known phenomenon in endurance athletes. Crucially, the authors show that this higher arrhythmia risk does not outweigh the substantial cardiovascular benefit: the net health balance strongly favors high fitness. Muscle strength did not share this atrial fibrillation downside, showing only protective associations across arrhythmia subtypes.
