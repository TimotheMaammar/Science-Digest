# Testosterone and marriage

# About 

Title : Influence of marital status on testosterone levels - A ten year follow-up of 1113 men

Link : https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28376340/

Researchers : Stine A. Holmboe, Lærke Priskorn, Niels Jørgensen, Niels E. Skakkebaek, Allan Linneberg, Anders Juul, Anna-Maria Andersson

# Synthesis

A landmark longitudinal study from Denmark followed 1113 men aged 30-60 over a 10-year period using the MONICA cohort, with baseline data collected between 1982 and 1984. By tracking intra-individual testosterone changes over time rather than simply comparing married vs. single men at a single point, the study directly addresses the classic chicken-and-egg problem: do men with lower testosterone tend to marry, or does marriage itself cause testosterone to drop?

The results clearly favor the causal direction. Men who got married during the follow-up period showed the steepest decline in testosterone, while men who divorced showed the smallest decline or even a relative increase. Men whose marital status remained stable fell in between. Crucially, LH levels did not differ by marital status, meaning testicular production capacity was unaffected, pointing to a central regulatory mechanism at the hypothalamic-pituitary level rather than a primary testicular effect.

The biological mechanisms remain unresolved. Leading hypotheses include adjustment of the HPG axis to a new social context, cortisol-mediated suppression linked to chronic stress from cohabitation, and lifestyle changes following marriage such as altered sleep, diet, or physical activity. The study does not distinguish between married men with and without children, nor between levels of parental involvement, leaving open questions that later work on fatherhood and pair bonding has begun to address.
