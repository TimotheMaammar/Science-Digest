# IQ and job performance

## About

Title : The validity and utility of selection methods in personnel psychology: practical and theoretical implications of 100 years of research findings

Link : https://www.researchgate.net/publication/309203898

Researchers : Frank L. Schmidt, In-Sue Oh, Jonathan A. Shaffer

Date : 10/2016

## Synthesis

A comprehensive update to the landmark Schmidt & Hunter (1998) paper, now integrating 100 years of personnel selection research across 31 predictors of job performance and 16 predictors of training performance. The central finding is that General Mental Ability (GMA, synonymous with general intelligence or IQ) is the single strongest predictor of job performance, with an average validity of r = 0.65 for medium-complexity jobs, which represent 62% of all jobs in the US economy. This figure is substantially higher than the r = 0.51 reported in 1998, due to a more accurate correction for indirect range restriction introduced by Hunter et al. (2006).

The most practically important finding is the incremental validity analysis. Most selection methods currently popular in business add little or nothing once GMA is already accounted for: work sample tests, job knowledge tests, assessment centers, peer ratings, and GPA all show near-zero incremental validity over GMA, because their predictive power largely overlaps with it. The two combinations that do stand out are GMA combined with an integrity test (composite validity = 0.78) and GMA combined with a structured interview (composite validity = 0.76). Graphology and emotional intelligence measures show negligible validity. Years of education predicts job performance at only r = 0.10.

The paper also clarifies the causal mechanism. GMA primarily affects job performance indirectly, through faster and deeper acquisition of job-specific knowledge. Job experience works the same way but plateaus after roughly five years, after which additional tenure produces little further improvement. GMA, by contrast, continues to predict performance indefinitely. Conscientiousness operates through the same knowledge-acquisition pathway, explaining why integrity tests, which partly measure conscientiousness, are the only non-cognitive predictor with meaningful incremental validity.
