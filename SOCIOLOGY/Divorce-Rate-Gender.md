# Divorce rates by couple sex composition

## About

Title : The demographics of same-sex marriages in Norway and Sweden

Link : https://link.springer.com/article/10.1353/dem.2006.0001

Researchers : Gunnar Andersson, Turid Noack, Ane Seierstad, Harald Weedon-Fekjaer

Date : 02/2006

## Synthesis

The most rigorous early study on same-sex union stability, built on complete national population registers from Norway and Sweden rather than surveys, which makes it far more reliable than the self-reported or law-firm data that circulate in popular articles. It analyzed every registered partnership formed in both countries through the early 2000s. The core finding established what has since become the most replicated result in this field: divorce-risk patterns are broadly similar across couple types, but divorce-risk levels are considerably higher in same-sex marriages than in different-sex marriages, and within same-sex couples, female-female couples divorce substantially more than male-male couples. The consistent hierarchy across Scandinavia, the US, and Canada is female-female > different-sex, with male-male couples often the most stable, sometimes matching or even falling below heterosexual rates.

Later register data refined this picture in an important way. A follow-up by Kolk and Andersson (2020) covering two decades of Swedish data found that the elevated divorce risk has converged over time: male same-sex marriages now show divorce risks essentially identical to different-sex marriages, while only female couples remain clearly more divorce-prone. This temporal shift matters because much of the early elevation was a cohort effect: the first wave of same-sex marriages involved older partners marrying after long pre-legal relationships, with no template for same-sex marriage, all of which inflated early dissolution. As the institution normalized, the gap narrowed for men but persisted for women.

The most cited explanation for the female-female pattern connects to a separate but robust finding: women initiate the majority of divorces. Michael Rosenfeld's analysis of the nationally representative How Couples Meet and Stay Together survey found that women initiated 69% of heterosexual divorces, while non-marital breakups were gender-neutral (men and women equally likely to end them). Married women also reported lower relationship satisfaction than married men, whereas dating partners reported similar satisfaction. Rosenfeld attributes this to the "stalled gender revolution," the persistence of unequal expectations (housework, childcare, surname) specifically within the institution of marriage rather than in relationships generally. A female-female couple effectively doubles the presence of the higher-initiating partner, which is the leading hypothesis for why lesbian couples show the highest dissolution rates, alongside differing emotional expectations and faster commitment timelines.
